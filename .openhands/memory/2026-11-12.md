### What was actually wrong (the expensive bit)

The core was told its void by a fitted line `void = 0.05 + 0.08*(power-1)`. The
nominal value 0.05 was **~7× too low** — the plant already carried the sourced
numbers (10,000 kg/s, 320 kJ/kg, ~15% quality) but the core ignored them.

Once the void was derived correctly (`ChannelCooling`, core-average ≈ 0.355), the
core **ran away** at nominal. Root cause: the derived lattice void worth is
**+0.086 dk/dvoid, 4.3× the sourced INSAG-7 +0.020**. The old fitted slope had been
*hiding* this — two errors (slope too shallow × worth too strong) were cancelling
to give a stable-by-accident net of −0.005. This is the classic "two bugs cancel"
trap; deriving one half exposed the other.

Fix per the mission rule (a sourced number beats a computed one): scale the void
DEPENDENCE to the source. `RbmkXsec.VOID_WORTH_SCALE = 0.233`, a blend of the
derived cell toward its zero-void baseline (`base + f*(cell-base)`), so the SHAPE
stays derived physics and only the MAGNITUDE is set to INSAG-7. Result: dk/dvoid
= +0.0222 (1.1× INSAG-7), core holds nominal (1.2% drift/30 s).

### The emergent coupling (the payoff)

`PlantSystem` now feeds the core its **actual** pump flow
(`UnifiedReactorCore.setCoolantFlowKgS`), and takes the outlet quality back from
`u.cooling().outletQuality()`. So a pump trip raises void AND power with **no
accident flag** — flow halved → P 1.00→1.11, void 0.355→0.528. That is the
flow→void→reactivity loop the whole project exists to show, now derived.

### CORRECTION (same day, later session): that "doubles" claim was WRONG

Measured it properly. The axial coupling does NOT double the void worth:

| void coupling | dk/dvoid |
|---|---|
| core average | +0.0222 |
| full axial profile | +0.0248 |

Only 12% higher. The real defect was a **seed inconsistency**: the core was seeded
at the cosine-void profile (mean 0.355) but the live loop runs at the flux-shape
void (mean ~0.64). Seeded at 0.355, running at 0.64 → starts badly super-critical
and drifts up +19%/30 s. THAT was the "runaway."

Fix: seed at the self-consistent fixed point. `UnifiedReactorCore.selfConsistentVoid()`
iterates (void profile from energy balance) × (flux shape that void produces) to
convergence at construction (3 static passes, ~5 s). Axial coupling is now the
**default**. Nominal hold went from 1.2% drift to **0.0%** over 30 s.

Two more findings from the diagnosis:
- A **uniform-material** core gives a **flat** axial flux, not a cosine → the axial
  BC is perfectly reflective. That is a legitimate stand-in for the axial graphite
  reflector (the RBMK ends are reflected, not bare). I TRIED a leakage BC (virtual
  zero-flux end nodes) and it made the loop WORSE (drift to 1.12 vs hold at 0.996),
  so I removed the experiment entirely. Lesson: reflective axial BC is correct here;
  the bottom-peaked shape comes from the ROD geometry, not a bare-end BC.
- Optimised `setAxialState`: one `RbmkXsec.fuelCell` per axial node (28), reused
  across all 1,872 channels, instead of 52k lookups per step.
